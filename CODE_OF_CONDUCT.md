# Code of Conduct — LibreVMM

## Our Philosophy

LibreVMM exists because open source software should be free — not just in license, but in spirit. Free to use, free to contribute to, and free from the hostility that has driven contributors away from other projects. Every person who files an issue, submits a pull request, or asks a question in a discussion has taken a step toward making this project better. That step deserves to be met with respect.

This Code of Conduct exists to protect that environment.

---

## Our Standards

We expect all participants — contributors, maintainers, and community members alike — to uphold the following:

### ✅ Encouraged Behavior

- **Welcoming language** — Greet new contributors warmly, regardless of their experience level.
- **Constructive feedback** — Critique ideas, not people. Explain *why* something doesn't fit the project's scope rather than simply dismissing it.
- **Patience** — Not everyone who files an issue is a developer. Treat non-technical contributors with the same respect as seasoned engineers.
- **Transparency** — When declining a feature request or closing an issue, provide a clear, reasoned explanation.
- **Humility** — Expertise is not a license to belittle. The most knowledgeable contributors are often the most approachable ones.

### ❌ Unacceptable Behavior

- **Technical elitism** — Dismissing, ridiculing, or belittling contributors on the basis of their skill level, background, or the perceived simplicity of their suggestion.
- **Condescension** — Responding to feature requests or bug reports in a manner that makes the reporter feel foolish for asking.
- **Hostility** — Aggressive, demeaning, or inflammatory language in any project space, including issues, pull requests, discussions, and commit messages.
- **Gatekeeping** — Using technical authority to shut down community voices rather than engage with them in good faith.
- **Retaliation** — Taking action against a contributor for raising a concern or disagreeing with a maintainer's decision.

---

## On Feature Requests

LibreVMM welcomes feature suggestions from everyone. A suggestion may be declined — scope, complexity, and maintainability are all valid considerations — but **the manner of that decline matters**.

Closing an issue with a reasoned explanation is responsible maintainership.  
Closing an issue with ridicule is not.

If a feature does not fit the project's current direction, contributors will be told so clearly, kindly, and without judgment.

---

## Enforcement

Violations of this Code of Conduct will be handled by the project maintainer. Depending on severity, consequences may include:

1. **A written warning** — issued publicly or privately, with a clear explanation of the violation.
2. **Temporary suspension** — from issues, pull requests, and discussions for a defined period.
3. **Permanent ban** — reserved for repeated violations, deliberate hostility, or behavior that materially harms the community.

Bans are not issued lightly, but they are issued without hesitation when warranted. The health of this community takes precedence over any individual's contributions, regardless of their standing or reputation.

### A Note on Reputation

Prominence in the open source community, whether as a maintainer of another project or otherwise, does not confer special treatment here. All participants are held to the same standard. Past conduct in other communities may be considered when evaluating patterns of behavior.

---

## Reporting

If you witness or experience a violation of this Code of Conduct, please contact the project maintainer directly. All reports will be reviewed promptly and handled with discretion.

Reports made in good faith will never be used against the reporter.

---

## Final Word

LibreVMM is built on the belief that good software comes from good communities. If you are here to build, to improve, and to collaborate — you are welcome here, unconditionally.

If you are here to dominate, gatekeep, or demean — this is not the project for you.

*This Code of Conduct is a living document and may be revised as the project grows.*
